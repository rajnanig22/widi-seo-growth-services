For about a year, I worked on growth for two connected AI software products. For one of them, Domain Rating went from **5 to 38** in under two months. We didn't pay for a single link.

That number gets attention, so I want to be clear about what was behind it, and what it doesn't prove.

## What we actually did

Nothing clever. Mostly four habits, repeated every week.

**Personal pitches, no templates.** Every email went to one editor or site owner, about their site. If I couldn't say why a link made sense for their readers, I didn't send it.

**Offering something real instead of money.** Guest content, useful resources, data they could use. The question was always what the other site gets out of it, not just what we get.

**Saying no to bad sites.** Every placement got checked before it went live. One site looked fine on the surface, decent metrics, relevant topic. Then I looked at where its other outbound links went. Gambling sites. We passed. A link from a site like that can cost more than it gives.

**Declining barter deals.** Plenty of people offered link swaps: you link to me, I link to you. It's fast, and it's exactly the kind of pattern search engines are built to discount. We said no, even when it slowed things down.

By the time I handed the program over, that product had **19 live placements** and **15 conversations still in progress**. Slow, one relationship at a time. But every one of those links was something I'd be comfortable explaining to anyone.

## What the number doesn't prove

Here's the honest part. Domain Rating is not a Google metric. It's a score from Ahrefs, an SEO tool, and [it's calculated from links only](https://ahrefs.com/blog/domain-rating/). It doesn't look at search traffic, rankings, or whether anyone bought anything.

So a jump from 5 to 38 tells you the backlink profile got much stronger, much faster. It doesn't tell you how many more people found the product, or how many signed up because of it. I don't have a clean answer to that, and I'd be suspicious of anyone who claimed one from DR alone.

It's also [a relative score](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/1409408-what-is-domain-rating-dr), compared against every other site in Ahrefs' database. Going from 5 to 38 is much easier than going from 50 to 83. I wouldn't promise this pace to a site that's already strong.

## Why I still think it matters

A new product with a DR of 5 is close to invisible. Even good content struggles to rank when almost nothing points to it. Getting the link profile out of that zone is often the step that lets everything else, content, technical fixes, start to pay off.
