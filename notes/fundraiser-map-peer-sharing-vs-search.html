Getting an Issue Found Isn't Always About Search

Widi Ginanjar · SEO & Growth · July 30, 2026 · 4 min read

The bottom line: search and peer sharing solve two different problems, not the same problem twice. Most of what's on this site is about search: fixing what stops a page from ranking, structuring content so an AI tool can find it. Search works when someone already knows enough to go looking. It does nothing for the person who doesn't know the thing exists yet, and has no reason to type anything into a search bar at all.

I came across a clear example of that second, harder problem on Reddit. A small nonprofit built a fundraiser as a map instead of a normal donation page. Each US state had its own dollar goal, and the state lit up once it was funded. They also shared, publicly, which channels actually worked: cold email and generic social posts brought in almost nothing. Peer sharing, people posting the campaign and their own friends responding, brought in most of the money. I can't verify the exact figures from that post well enough to repeat them here, but the pattern lines up with what the wider data shows.

The ask that spread wasn't an ask for money

A normal donation page asks for money directly. The map gave people a reason to talk about the campaign with someone else first. "My state's only at 20 percent" is a sentence a person can say out loud to a friend. "Please donate to our year-end campaign" isn't.

That's a small design difference, but it moved who was doing the asking. Instead of the nonprofit reaching out to donors directly, donors ended up reaching out to their own friends. The organisation built the structure. The supporters did the actual asking, in their own words, to people who already trusted them.

Same underlying problem as findability, solved through a different route

The rest of this site talks about findability through search: an NGO writes in mission language, a donor or funder searches in plain language, and the two don't meet unless the site is built to bridge them. This is the same problem, one level upstream. Nobody was ever going to search for this specific campaign, because nobody knew it existed yet. The only way in was someone they already trusted, sharing it first.

This is also why donor and campaign engagement belongs next to SEO, not apart from it. Both answer the same question: how does something true and worth knowing actually reach the people it's for. Sometimes the fix is a canonical tag. Sometimes it's turning a fundraising ask into something a supporter actually wants to repeat.

Neither replaces the other. A nonprofit with strong search visibility and no shareable story still misses the person who'd never think to search. A nonprofit with a shareable campaign and an invisible website still loses the person who looks it up afterward to check it's real.
